## The Engineer Who Became a Manager

For a long time, my world was defined by code. As a Software and Data Engineer, success was measurable: tests passed, pipelines ran, latency dropped. But as I transitioned into Project Management, the variables became human, and the "compilation errors" became communication gaps.

Having been in the trenches of engineering, I have a distinct advantage: I know _how_ the work gets done. But knowing how to build the software isn't the same as knowing how to manage the project.

## Why PMP?

I've been "doing" project management for a while now. I know Agile, I know Scrum, and I know how to ship. But I realized I wanted to formalize that experience. I wanted the vocabulary and the framework to back up my instincts. That's where the Project Management Professional (PMP) certification comes in.

## Evaluating the Options

When I first looked into certification, my instinct was to go "academic."

### The University of Washington Program

I seriously considered the UW Project Management Certificate. It's a 9-month program with a great reputation.

- **Pros**: Structured, networking opportunities, prestige.
- **Cons**: **Expensive** and time-consuming.

Ultimately, looking at the ROI, I couldn't justify the cost when my goal was primarily certification and competence, not necessarily a new degree.

### The Self-Directed Path

I decided to take control of my own learning path. This allows me to move at my own pace—which, knowing me, will likely be faster than a 9-month cohort.

## My Chosen Resources: Andrew Ramdayal

After scouring Reddit threads and reviews, one name kept coming up: **Andrew Ramdayal**.

I've purchased his PMP Exam Prep book and am pairing it with his **Udemy course**.

### Why Andrew Ramdayal?

1. **The "Mindset"**: Everyone talks about his focus on the "PMP Mindset"—teaching you how to _think_ like a manager for the exam, rather than just memorizing inputs and outputs.
2. **Practicality**: He breaks down complex PMBOK concepts into digestible, real-world examples.
3. **Cost-Effective**: Compared to a university program, this route delivers high value for a fraction of the price.

## Next Steps

I'm starting the Udemy course this week. My plan is to:

1. Complete the 35 contact hours required for the exam application.
2. Read the book chapter-by-chapter alongside the videos.
3. Drill practice questions until I'm dreaming in "Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring/Controlling, and Closing."

I'll be sharing updates here as I progress. If you're an engineer thinking about moving into management, or just curious about the PMP process, stay tuned.
